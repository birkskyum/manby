## Against tylertoo

[tylertoo](https://github.com/geoparquet-io/tylertoo) makes tiles of its own, not tippecanoe's ([how](../../why/#tylertoo)), so this compares time on the same features rather than the same output. Both made the same zoom levels with a 500 kB tile size limit.

<BarChart chart={charts.tylertooTime} />

<BarChart chart={charts.tylertooMemory} />

- tylertoo is faster on the tracts, three times at z7 and 1.2 times at z12. It fits each tile on its own, where tippecanoe, and so manby, keeps one threshold for a whole zoom level and makes the level again until every tile fits. That is only part of the difference. manby fits each tile on its own too with [`--as-needed-per-tile`](../../guides/per-tile/), and is then 1.2 times faster on the tracts.
- Its tiles hold more. At z5 they have 61,746 tract polygons, where tippecanoe's have 17,094, and manby's with `--as-needed-per-tile` 67,163.
- At its defaults it thins points more, keeping 1,094 at z0 where tippecanoe keeps 16,384.

## How these were measured

- **tippecanoe** is the C++ built with its own Makefile. Its runs and manby's come from separate sessions on the same machine, as tippecanoe's do not change with manby's version.
- **tylertoo** is its main branch, 6c3cec4, as its release 0.8.0 does not have the size options yet. It read GeoParquet made from the GeoJSON with DuckDB, and alternated with manby in one session, so manby's figures there differ a little from those above. manby took the same time from FlatGeobuf as from GeoJSON.
- **The data.** The points are 4 million clustered random points (556 MB), or 40 million (5.6 GB), from `bench/points.py`. The roads are the 17,522 US primary roads of TIGER/Line 2024, 122,725 tiles at z14. The tracts are the 85,186 census tracts of the Census cartographic boundaries 2023, 312,073 tiles at z12, and 20 times over, moved north and south by `bench/repeat.py`.
- **The scripts** are in [`bench/`](https://github.com/birkskyum/manby/tree/main/bench).

### The commands

Each command runs as `tippecanoe -f -o out.pmtiles` or `manby -f -o out.pmtiles`, followed by these options.

| Benchmark | Options and input |
| --- | --- |
| Points, dropping the densest as needed | `-zg --drop-densest-as-needed -P points4m.geojsonl` |
| Points, clustered within 10 pixels, values summed | `-zg -r1 --cluster-distance=10 --cluster-densest-as-needed --accumulate-attribute=value:sum -P points4m.geojsonl` |
| Points, clustering the densest as needed | `-zg --cluster-densest-as-needed -P points4m.geojsonl` |
| Points, clustered at every zoom, values summed | `-zg -r1 --cluster-densest-as-needed --accumulate-attribute=value:sum -P points4m.geojsonl` |
| Roads to z14 | `-z14 -Z0 --drop-densest-as-needed -P tl_2024_us_primaryroads.geojsonl` |
| Tracts to z12 | `-z12 -Z0 --coalesce-densest-as-needed -P cb_2023_us_tract_500k.geojsonl` |
| Tracts, coalescing the densest as needed | `-zg --coalesce-densest-as-needed -P cb_2023_us_tract_500k.geojsonl` |
| 40 million points | `--memory-budget=1G -zg --drop-densest-as-needed -P points40m.geojsonl` (budget for manby only) |
| The tracts 20 times over | `--memory-budget=1G -zg --coalesce-densest-as-needed -P tracts20.geojsonl` (budget for manby only) |

tylertoo ran as `tylertoo tiles` with `--max-zoom 6 --size-valve drop` for the points, `--max-zoom 14 --size-valve drop` for the roads, and `--max-zoom 12` or `--max-zoom 7` for the tracts, whose default size valve merges the densest polygons.
